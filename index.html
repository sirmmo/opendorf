OpenDorf

Grow a medieval realm one hex tile at a time.

  1. Lay tiles next to your realm. Roads and rivers must carry on, and a beach must run on into more beach or open water. Land may meet the sea as a low cliff, and rivers may flow into the sea.
  2. Houses put their land to work. A house next to a forest becomes a lumbermill, next to fields a windmill, by a river a watermill, under a mountain a mine, by the sea a watchtower, below hills a quarry and by a marsh an herb garden. Bigger lands feed more of them.
  3. Villages grow. Three houses together dig a well. Then come a tavern (5), a market (7), a church (10) and finally a castle (14). A village with a lumbermill and a mine gets a blacksmith; with a blacksmith and a watchtower within two tiles, Barracks; with Barracks and a market, an archery range. A meadow beside a church becomes its churchyard.
  4. Hills, marsh and bridges. Marsh only lies where it touches a river or the sea, and a marsh of six draws a witch's hut. A bridge takes a road square across a river.
  5. The wild. Bandits may camp in a big forest, and wolves den in a mountain, far from any village. They keep the woodcutters or miners away until a watchtower or Barracks stands within two tiles and sees them off. The first range of three mountains hides a dungeon: tap its mouth to explore it. Tap a tavern to run it as an inn in OpenInn.
  6. Ruins of an older realm turn up now and then. A house next to them becomes a dig site (one for the first ruin, then one more for every two). A village with a dig site and a tavern or market founds an Archaeological Society; once it also has a church, the Society opens an Academy.
  7. Every building and royal charter brings new tiles. When the last tile is laid, your realm is complete.
